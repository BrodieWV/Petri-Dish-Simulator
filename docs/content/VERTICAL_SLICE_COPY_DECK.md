## Guided experiment card

**The Comfortable Range**  
Help a fast-growing culture find the temperature range where it grows best.

- Lesson: Temperature tolerance
- Organism: Rapid Bacterium
- Medium: Nutrient Agar
- Duration: About 5 minutes
- Difficulty: Beginner

## Setup introduction

**Every culture has conditions it prefers.**

This dish begins slightly too cold. Observe its growth, adjust the temperature, and learn how heat can also change moisture.

## Tutorial prompts

### Prompt 1 — First observation

The colony is growing, but only slowly. Check the condition panel to see what is limiting it.

Buttons:

- Show me
- Continue

### Prompt 2 — Temperature control

The dish is cooler than this culture prefers. Increase the temperature gradually and watch the growth edge.

### Prompt 3 — Approaching the range

Growth is increasing. Keep the temperature steady for a moment so the culture can respond.

### Prompt 4 — Preferred range reached

The colony is growing well. You have found a comfortable temperature range.

### Prompt 5 — Heat demonstration

A temporary heater fault has made the dish too hot. Watch how the culture responds before correcting it.

### Prompt 6 — Correct the heat

The colony is stressed. Return the dish to the comfortable range.

### Prompt 7 — Moisture effect

The temperature is safe again, but the dish lost moisture while it was hot. Add a small amount of water.

### Prompt 8 — Recovery

The environment is suitable again. Recovery takes time, so watch for the growth edge to become active.

### Prompt 9 — Completion

The culture is healthy and has covered more than half the dish. The experiment is complete.

## Condition labels and explanations

### Growing well

Conditions are suitable and the colony edge is expanding.

### Growing slowly

The culture is viable, but one or more conditions are limiting growth.

### Too cold

Low temperature is slowing the processes needed for growth.

### Too hot

High temperature has stopped growth and is increasing stress.

### Too dry

The medium does not contain enough available moisture for strong growth.

### Nutrient limited

Local food resources are becoming scarce.

### Recovering

Conditions have improved, but the culture needs time before normal growth returns.

### Dying

The culture has remained outside its survival conditions for too long.

## Discovery 1

### A Comfortable Range

**Observation**  
The colony grew fastest when the dish stayed between 26 °C and 30 °C.

**Likely cause**  
The culture’s growth processes worked efficiently within this temperature range.

**Why it matters**  
Living things usually have a range of conditions where they function best. Outside that range, growth slows before survival necessarily becomes impossible.

**Try next**  
Restart the experiment and compare growth at 18 °C, 28 °C, and 33 °C.

**Accuracy label**  
Educational simplification

## Discovery 2

### Heat Can Dry a Dish

**Observation**  
Moisture fell faster while the dish was overheated.

**Likely cause**  
Higher temperature increased evaporation from the medium.

**Why it matters**  
Environmental variables can affect each other. Temperature does not only act directly on the organism; it can also change the habitat.

**Try next**  
Compare a warm dish with low airflow to one with stronger airflow when that control becomes available.

**Accuracy label**  
Observed relationship represented through a simplified model

## Success outcome

### Culture successful

You found a suitable temperature range, corrected heat stress, and restored moisture before the colony collapsed.

**What happened**

- Cool conditions slowed early growth.
- Comfortable warmth accelerated expansion.
- Excess heat stopped growth and increased drying.
- Restored temperature and moisture allowed gradual recovery.

## Tooltips

### Temperature

Controls how warm the dish is. Each organism has preferred, limiting, and dangerous ranges.

### Moisture

Shows how much usable water remains in the medium. Heat and airflow can increase moisture loss.

### Coverage

The portion of the dish currently occupied by visible colony growth.

### Health

A combined estimate of stress, damage, and recovery across the culture.

### Limiting factor

The condition currently having the strongest effect on growth.

### Experiment seed

A number that reproduces the same starting layout and random events.

## Accuracy labels

### Observed relationship

Represents a broadly established biological or physical relationship.

### Educational simplification

Directionally realistic, but compressed or combined to make the game understandable.

### Gameplay abstraction

Created mainly to support clear decisions or enjoyable play.

### Fictional

Not intended to represent real biology.
