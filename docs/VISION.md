# Vision

## Product vision

Create a bright, tactile ecosystem sandbox where players learn how living systems respond to their environment by experimenting rather than reading a textbook.

The first release is an accessible mobile game. Its architecture should later support more advanced biology, chemistry, ecology, microscopy, and classroom content.

## Audience

Primary audiences are children and teenagers interested in science, casual simulation players, parents, and educators. Secondary audiences include biology students, microscopy enthusiasts, and optimisation-focused players.

## Emotional goals

The player should feel curiosity, ownership, concern, surprise, and satisfaction when they correctly infer why a culture grows, changes, stabilises, or collapses.

## Product promise

Every visible change should have an understandable cause, even when the player has not discovered it yet.

## Long-term product ladder

1. Petri Dish — simplified organisms, environmental controls, challenges, discoveries, and collection.
2. Petri Terrarium — layered substrates, airflow zones, condensation, and richer food webs.
3. Microbial Ecosystem Lab — interacting microbial groups, chemical gradients, genetics, and microscopy.
4. Education Edition — teacher scenarios, learning objectives, worksheets, and progress reporting.

## Success criteria

A new player can begin an experiment within two minutes, sees meaningful change within the first minute, and can explain at least one cause of success or failure.
