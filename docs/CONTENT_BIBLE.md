# Content Bible

## Tone

Curious, observational, playful, and scientifically respectful. Avoid medical horror, punishment-heavy wording, false species precision, and long textbook passages during active play.

## Organism growth references

Detailed species-specific growth habits, colony appearance, mass-growth speed, spread speed, ageing, stress response, exposure-style differences, and gameplay progression are maintained in:

- [Bacterial Species Growth and Appearance Guide](content/BACTERIAL_SPECIES_GROWTH_GUIDE.md)
- [Fungal Species Growth and Appearance Guide](content/FUNGAL_SPECIES_GROWTH_GUIDE.md)

These guides define the visual target and simulation identity for each organism. Values described as fast, medium, or slow are relative gameplay tiers rather than laboratory timing claims.

## Organism archetypes

### Bacillus subtilis

A stylised model bacterium for exploring colony growth, resource limits, drying, temperature response, dormancy, and spore survival. Its simulation values are educational gameplay tuning, not strain-specific measurements or cultivation guidance.

### Deinococcus radiodurans

A compact, pigmented bacterial colony used to explore accumulated damage, paused growth, energy-dependent repair, and recovery of existing biomass.

### Rhodopseudomonas palustris

A thin, coloured bacterial film used to explore environmental switching between metabolic modes. Light, oxygen, moisture, and local resources affect pigment, thickness, and spread independently.

### Streptomyces violaceoruber

A filamentous soil bacterium used to explore branching growth, substrate mycelium, aerial sporulation, pigment development, and chemical competition.

### Neurospora crassa

A rapidly spreading filamentous fungus used to make biological rhythms visible through repeated vegetative-growth and orange-sporulation bands.

### Pleurotus ostreatus

A wood-decay fungus used to explore substrate penetration, network consolidation, environmental phase changes, and three-dimensional fruiting.

### Trichoderma harzianum

A fast, green-sporulating fungus used to explore competition, fungal detection, overgrowth, and mycoparasitic behaviour.

### Pilobolus crystallinus

A dung-associated fungus used to explore phototropic aiming, pressure accumulation, projectile spore dispersal, and delayed establishment at a distant landing point.

### Schizophyllum commune

A wood-decay fungus used to explore persistent mycelial networks, compatibility encounters, moisture-responsive fruit bodies, and split-gill architecture.

### Filamentous Fungus

A non-species-specific fallback archetype with branching threads and radial fans. It teaches hyphal growth, moisture, and substrate structure when a named species profile is not selected.

### Slime Mould

Bright moving networks; teaches gradients, distributed behaviour, and efficient paths.

### Saccharomyces cerevisiae

A stylised, non-strain-specific budding yeast culture for comparing temperature and nutrient responses. It forms rounded, compact clusters and spreads more slowly than the current Bacillus subtilis gameplay profile. The simulation does not model individual budding cells, fermentation, pseudohyphae, sporulation, or diagnostic appearance.

## Exposure styles

Species selection determines how an organism behaves after deposition. Exposure style determines where its founding cells, spores, or inoculum begin.

### Needle or point touch

- Creates one concentrated founding location.
- Best for displaying radial age zoning and a single colony's full lifecycle.
- Filamentous species produce one main branching network.

### Droplet

- Distributes many founders throughout a circular or irregular wet footprint.
- Internal microcolonies begin simultaneously and later merge.
- The exposed footprint may cloud, pigment, or develop hyphae before expanding beyond the original droplet edge.

### Streak

- Deposits founders along a dragged line with density generally decreasing along the stroke.
- Dense areas may become continuous growth while sparse areas form isolated colonies.
- Branching organisms can extend sideways beyond the original streak.

### Spray or airborne exposure

- Places many scattered founders with random spacing and local density.
- Produces multiple colonies that may remain separate, compete, or merge.
- Best for contamination and mixed-colony scenarios.

### Contact print

- Transfers a spatial pattern from a touched object, fingerprint, tool, leaf, or other surface.
- Growth initially preserves the contact geometry before species-specific spreading distorts it.

### Mixed into substrate

- Places founders throughout the medium or three-dimensional substrate rather than only on the surface.
- Suitable for wood, soil, particulate substrates, yeast-like populations, and scenarios involving hidden colonisation.

### Projectile spore impact

- Creates a distant adhesive founding point after a dispersal event.
- Used especially by Pilobolus crystallinus.
- Landing and successful establishment are separate events.

## Media

Nutrient agar, low-nutrient agar, wood-chip substrate, and moist soil gel.

The medium controls resource type, resource density, moisture retention, surface firmness, depth access, transparency, and structural pathways. It must not replace the separate exposure-style selection.

## Discovery categories

Growth, stress, resources, tolerance, competition, dormancy, recovery, pattern formation, contamination, intervention, metabolic switching, dispersal, compatibility, and fruiting.

## Discovery template

Observation; likely cause; why it matters; try next; accuracy label.
